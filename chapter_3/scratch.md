**3.1 B**

$$ u = (1, 2, 1) $$

$$ u =
\begin{bmatrix}{}1\\2\\1\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}{}v_1\\v_2\\v_3\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix}{}0\\0\\0\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$ v_1 + 2v_2 + v_3 = 0 $$

Pick 2 free values, and determine the third:

$$
v_1 = 1 \\
v_3 = 1 \\
\to v_2 = -1 \\
$$

$$ v = \begin{bmatrix}{}1\\-1\\1\end{bmatrix} $$

This is just one vector that lies within $V_2$, setting $v_1, v_2, v_3$ above
and as long as they satisfy the derived equation of equaling $0$ will be within
the subspace $V_2$. Note that setting it to $0$ essentially is saying it passes
through the $\mathbf{0}$ vector, which all subspaces must have and all vectors
within all spaces/subspaces must do as a rule.

3.1B

Later on Strang makes mention of the use of the wording "all combination of"
followed by defined vectors or matrices, as well as the wording "all solutions
of" followed by some equation. It is important to note that the "combinations"
wording refers to a description of the vector space as it _spans_ across the
overall space, and the "solutions" of wording refers to a description of how
that subspace is _constrained_ within the overall space.
